The Trip Begins at Home

Get River-Ready

A strong, confident crew has more fun and stays safe. Here's how to show up ready to paddle.

Non-Negotiable

Pass the Swimmer Test

To paddle whitewater, every crew member must be a confident "Swimmer". This is the single most important thing to nail before the trip.

🏊 The BSA Swimmer Test

Jump feet-first into water over your head, swim 75 yards in a strong stroke (front crawl, breaststroke, sidestroke, or trudgen), then swim 25 yards using an easy resting backstroke — 100 yards total, non-stop — and finish by resting by floating. Practice at the pool until it's easy.

Build the Skills

What to Practice

You'll get full guide instruction at the river — but the more you show up knowing, the more fun the big rapids are.

🛶

Paddle Strokes

Get comfortable with the forward stroke, back stroke, and draw. Any flatwater kayaking or canoeing near home builds the muscle memory you'll use every day.

🤝

Paddle as a Team

Whitewater is a team sport. Practice listening for commands and paddling together in rhythm — a crew that moves as one runs the cleanest lines.

🛟

Wear Your PFD

Get used to a snug life jacket. Learn the whitewater swimmer's position — on your back, feet up and downstream — so it's second nature if you take a swim.

Build Your Engine

Get in Paddling Shape

Paddling all day takes core, shoulder, and grip strength — plus all-around endurance. Start a few months out and build steadily.

1

Cardio Base

Run, bike, or swim a few times a week. Good endurance means you're still smiling on the last rapid of the day.

2

Upper Body & Core

Push-ups, planks, pull-ups, and rows build the paddling muscles. A strong core is what powers every stroke.

3

Get on the Water

Any kayaking, canoeing, or paddleboarding you can do before June pays off. Comfort in a boat is half the battle.

4

Break In Your Shoes

Wear your water shoes and river sandals on hikes and around the pool so there are zero blisters on day one.

☀️ Two Things Everyone Forgets

Sun and water. You'll be on open water all day — practice slathering on sunscoop-proof sunscreen and drinking water constantly, even when you're not thirsty. Dehydration and sunburn ruin river trips faster than any rapid.
