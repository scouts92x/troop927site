Before You Commit

Can I Go?

A Cavalcade is a horseback trek — so the rules are a little different (and a lot stricter) than a backpacking trek.

Read This First

The Two Hard Cutoffs

Philmont does not make exceptions to these. If you don't meet them at the medical recheck, you will not be allowed on the trail — so know them now, in 2028's planning stage.

⚖️ 1. You Must Meet the Height-to-Weight Limit

Philmont's Horse Department sets a maximum weight based on your height — it's a sliding scale, so shorter riders have lower limits (see the full chart below). The overall ceiling is 210 lb at 73"+ and just 125 lb at 56". Note: the Cavalcade Guidebook also states a firm 200 lb Cavalcade limit — so plan and train to the stricter figure for your height and confirm your exact number with Philmont when reserving. This protects the horses.

🤠 2. Mount & Dismount Unassisted — From the Ground

Regardless of height or weight, every participant must be able to independently mount and dismount a horse from the ground. Per the Cavalcade Guidebook, "participants who fail to mount unassisted from the ground will not be allowed to participate." Practice this well before you arrive.

The Full Checklist

Every Participation Requirement

Compiled from the Philmont Cavalcade page and the Cavalcade Guidebook (Guidebook to Adventure, p. 67).

1

Registered Scouting America Member & Age

Must be a registered member of Scouting America who will be 14 years old, or has completed 8th grade and is at least 13, prior to participation. Do not expect exceptions. A crew must have a majority of members under age 21.

2

Meet Your Height-to-Weight Limit

Your maximum weight depends on your height — see the official Horse Department chart below. Ranges from 125 lb at 56" up to 210 lb at 73"+. The Cavalcade Guidebook also cites a firm 200 lb limit, so plan to the stricter number for your height.

3

Mount Unassisted From the Ground

You must be able to get into the saddle by yourself, from the ground, every time.

4

Blood Pressure Under 140 / 90

Blood pressure without medication must be less than 140/90 for a participant to be permitted on the trail. This is verified at the Philmont medical recheck.

5

Annual Health & Medical Record (AHMR)

A completed AHMR (Parts A, B & C) is required. The physician's physical evaluation is valid through the end of the 12th month from the exam date — so time it to still be current in June 2028. A tetanus shot/booster within 10 years is required; a current measles vaccination is highly recommended.

6

Medical Review at the Ranch

Philmont staff physicians may deny backcountry access based on the medical recheck. Areas of concern include (but aren't limited to) heart disease, seizure disorder, sickle cell anemia, and hemophilia. Bring all emergency medication in original containers and know how to use it.

7

Wilderness First Aid + CPR (Crew Level)

Three people in the crew must be currently certified in Wilderness First Aid Basic and CPR. We'll line up training well before departure.

8

Physically Fit for the Saddle

Riders spend 3 to 8+ hours a day on horseback at 6,500–11,000 ft, plus side hikes on layover days. You must be able to sustain eight hours in the saddle, alert and balanced. Regular aerobic conditioning is expected (see the Prepare page).

9

Horsemanship Merit Badge (Recommended)

Philmont highly recommends every rider earn the Horsemanship Merit Badge before the trek. There's a big difference between eight hours in a Philmont saddle and an hour at a local stable.

10

Crew Size

A Cavalcade crew can run up to 15 participants (the max earns the "La Docena Adventurado" recognition). We need enough riders and the required adult leadership.

ℹ️ Bottom Line

If you can meet your height-to-weight limit, mount & dismount unassisted, keep BP under 140/90, complete your health record, and put in the conditioning — you're on track to ride with Troop 927 in 2028. Questions about your specific situation? Talk to a family physician and our crew advisor early.
